# Launch playbook

## Positioning

**Audience:** independent designers, developers, consultants, photographers, and service businesses preparing project proposals.

**Promise:** make a scope and price clear, present it beautifully, and keep documents on the device.

**Why this may resonate:** developers and freelancers repeatedly discuss the friction of producing proposals, controlling scope, and paying for document software. This is a hypothesis to validate with users, not evidence of existing traction.

## Before promotion

- Publish a tested HTTPS live demo and accurately document limitations.
- Validate the product on iOS Safari, Android Chrome, desktop Safari/Firefox/Chrome, and test print-to-PDF pagination.
- Run a security review and complete a license/trademark/name search.
- Add genuine screenshots made from the working app (use fictional data).
- Confirm all Git commits, author metadata and file contents are fit for public view; publish from a new clean repo, not old unrelated project history.

## A practical 30-day launch

**Week 1:** interview five to ten freelancers about their last proposal. Time the workflow, then fix the first major friction points.

**Week 2:** write useful guides: 'Scope exclusions that prevent surprises', 'How to price a small website proposal', and 'Deposit versus final milestone'. Share only where the community allows it.

**Week 3:** publish a short, real product demo and a release announcement. Submit to product directories with factual feature descriptions and no fake user counts.

**Week 4:** review anonymized, opt-in user feedback (not customer document data), improve issues and onboarding, and publish a transparent changelog.

## Metrics worth measuring ethically

Gather *voluntary* feedback on time-to-first-proposal, success rate for PDF export, reported clarity, accessibility, and bugs. Do not add silent analytics or pretend that repository stars equal customer value.

## Don't do this

Do not buy stars or reviews, automate mass follows, post unsolicited promotions, scrape private contact data, invent press coverage, or claim guaranteed business outcomes.
